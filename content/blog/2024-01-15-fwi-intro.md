# Introduction to Full Waveform Inversion for Medical Ultrasound

Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) is a high-resolution imaging technique originally developed in geophysics that uses the complete recorded wavefield to reconstruct quantitative tissue parameters. In medical ultrasound, FWI enables **sound-speed** and **attenuation** maps with sub-wavelength resolution.

## Why FWI for Medical Ultrasound?

Conventional ultrasound imaging (B-mode) provides qualitative contrast. FWI reconstructs quantitative maps that are directly interpretable for diagnosis. Key advantages:

1. **Quantitative biomarkers** — sound speed correlates with tissue composition
2. **High resolution** — exploits the full wavefield, not just first arrivals
3. **Multiparameter** — simultaneously reconstructs sound speed, density, and attenuation

## Key Challenges

1. **Computational cost**: Each iteration requires solving the wave equation on a GPU cluster
2. **Cycle skipping**: When the starting model is too far from the true tissue properties
3. **Ultrasound-specific physics**: Transducer directivity, finite bandwidth, and attenuation
